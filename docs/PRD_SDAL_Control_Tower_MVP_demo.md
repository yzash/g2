# PRD: SDAL Control Tower MVP demo

Oct 2, 2026 · @Yash Thakker · DevX Labs for Globe Telecom · Draft v0.1

## 1. Purpose

The demo shows the Head of Broadband what a working day in the CFS control tower looks like when four SDAL agents sit on top of the data Globe has already shared, before any live integration exists. It runs on Globe's real schemas (FSM work orders, ServiceNow outages, OLT→LCP→NAP topology, subscriber bases, WMS optical telemetry, Handyman diagnostics) filled with synthetic rows, so nothing shown is sanctioned operational data and nothing shown is a claim about Globe's numbers.

The demo must prove five things in twenty minutes:

1. One correlated view exists: Handyman signals, repair orders, SNOW outages, optical telemetry and alarm samples on one screen, across all eight territories. That is the control tower's own stated ask.
2. The platform detects a non-alarm fault before the current hourly cycle would, from ticket clustering and optical signatures, and explains why with evidence rows.
3. Every dispatch-eligible order gets a recommendation (dispatch, suppress, hold-and-recheck) with cited evidence, and a wrong suppression is architecturally impossible below the confidence floor.
4. The queue is ranked by arithmetic the operations lead can inspect, and any ranking can be explained on demand.
5. Customers inside a confirmed incident's blast radius get a draft notification package routed to a human approver; nothing is sent.

What the demo is not: a production system, an integration, or a baseline. Fidelity follows the Capability–Data Matrix in SOW v3; where a source is absent (planned works, power, consent), the demo shows the check labelled unavailable rather than hiding it.

## 2. The end state: one morning in the control tower

The demo is a narrated morning, 06:00 to 08:30 on a synthetic Tuesday, told through the four screens the operators would use. Every number on screen is generated; every column name is Globe's.

**06:00 - Shift opens.** The NOC lead opens the Control Tower. One map, all eight territories. Open repair orders from FSM, open SNOW outages, Handyman diagnostics from the last hour and optical health from WMS are already correlated on NAP. The feed-health strip shows every source arrived on time; a stalled feed shows as an alert, never as a quiet night.

**06:12 - A cluster forms that nobody has called an outage yet.** Four repair orders land on NAP CAV\_118\_L004\_N07B inside forty minutes. No alarm in any NMS. The Incident Investigator picks up the deterministic detector's signal (3 or more orders on one NAP in a rolling 6 hours), checks siblings under LCP CAV\_118\_L004, and finds the optical Rx on 11 of 14 gateways on that NAP dropped below -27 dBm at 05:48. It proposes incident SDAL-0412: NAP-level fibre fault, 14 lines inferred affected (4 called, 10 silent), support score 0.86. The NOC lead reads the evidence rows and confirms. Under today's hourly cycle this would have surfaced around 07:00.

**06:15 - The dispatch queue reacts.** The Dispatch Reviewer re-reviews every dispatch-eligible order. The four orders on the new incident get **suppress** (upstream fault; evidence: incident link plus optical drop). One order on a neighbouring NAP with a healthy optical reading and ModemStatus = Inactive/Modem not found in Handyman gets **dispatch** with a CPE-signature note. One order whose only evidence is a 12-hour-old Handyman check gets **hold-and-recheck, 2h**. The dispatcher accepts three and overrides one with a reason; the override lands in the adoption record. Planned-works and power checks show as unavailable in pilot, not as clear.

**06:30 - Queue re-ranks.** The Queue Prioritizer puts SDAL-0412 above an older OLT-level incident because the older one has a crew assigned and the new one breaches the six-hour window first. The operations lead asks why 0412 is above 0398 and gets the scoring inputs: elapsed time, lines affected, severity class, crew status.

**06:40 - The silent majority hears first.** The Notification agent resolves the 14 subscribers on the NAP from the subscriber tables, splits VIP/B2B from mass, checks contact policy (quiet hours, frequency cap; no consent source, so draft only), and builds a package carrying the restoration target from outage management, or a next-update time if none exists. The communications approver sees recipients, text and evidence, and approves the draft. Nothing is sent; the NG1 handoff is shown as the single governed write, disabled.

**07:10 - Restoration.** Optical Rx on the NAP returns to -20 dBm; the Investigator proposes closure; the NOC lead confirms. The adoption record shows the morning: one incident, four suppressions, one override, one draft notification, detection 48 minutes ahead of the hourly cycle, zero wrong suppressions, all with evidence IDs.

**07:30 - The question.** The Head of Broadband asks the surface in plain language: which NAPs had three or more repeat orders this week with no SNOW incident? The answer cites rows from the curated layer. This is the former UC6 shown as a capability of the same surface, not as a separate use case.

**Synthetic data generator (seeded, deterministic)**

A single Python script produces one synthetic day for Territory T5 and T7 (Davao and Cavite clusters, as they appear in Globe's own reporting) plus thin coverage of the other six territories so the national map is populated.

- Topology: 2 OLTs per cluster, 6 LCPs per OLT, 12 NAPs per LCP, 8 to 16 subscribers per NAP. NAP IDs follow Globe's naming pattern. Target: about 300 NAPs, about 3,500 subscribers.
- Subscribers: split postpaid and prepaid 60/40, prod\_id drawn from the real product mapping, 3 percent VIP/B2B flag (synthetic, since Globe's flag is unconfirmed), 2.6 percent with blank nap to mirror the real base.
- Gateways: one MAC per subscriber, 5-minute readings for 24 hours, healthy Rx between -19 and -21 dBm, noise plus/minus 0.4 dB, 2 percent of readings missing at random, raw vendor units written exactly as WMS writes them.
- Repair orders: background rate calibrated so the day looks like a normal day, with the standard Globe mix of statuses, delay codes and fix descriptions; 69 percent of fixdescription blank, as in the real file.
- Outages: 6 background SNOW incidents in various states, one of them a flapping NAP generating short repeat tickets, mirroring the real caveat.
- Handyman: one diagnostic per repair order plus 15 percent of subscribers running a self-serve check, templates and status strings copied from the dictionary.
- Seeded faults: the six scenarios in section 10, each injected at a fixed clock time so the demo is reproducible.
- Pseudonymisation: account numbers and service IDs are hashed tokens; names and phone numbers are never generated. This matches the pilot's PII design, where the agent runtime never sees contact fields.

The generator writes CSV and parquet in the same shapes Globe delivers, so the ingestion code that loads it is the code that loads Globe's files in week 2.

## 4. Agent 1: Incident Investigator (UC1)

The Investigator turns candidate signals into one deduplicated, explainable incident object; it never computes a score by inference and never creates an incident without a human confirmation.

**Inputs**

- Candidate signals from deterministic detectors running on the curated layer every 5 minutes: ticket-burst (3 or more open repair orders on one NAP, or 10 or more on one OLT, within a rolling 6 hours, Globe's own thresholds); optical-drop (median Rx on a NAP falls more than 6 dB below its 24-hour baseline across at least 60 percent of reporting gateways); reachability (more than 50 percent of a NAP's gateways stop reporting for two consecutive intervals); alarm (any NMS sample alarm on an OLT or LCP).
- Open SNOW incidents, so a signal already covered by an outage is linked, not duplicated.
- Handyman results in the last 60 minutes for the affected lines.

**Behaviour (fixed contract, implemented as tool calls in this order)**

1. ticket\_cluster\_read: fetch the orders behind the signal with cabinetid, lcpname, dpid, createdate, faultcode.
2. incident\_registry.search: same NAP, or same OLT, open or resolved in the last 6 hours. If a match exists, propose merge.
3. topology\_lookup: resolve NAP to LCP and OLT from master\_olt\_lcp\_nap; count sibling NAPs.
4. sibling\_health: for each sibling NAP, latest optical median and order count. Isolation hypothesis: if siblings are clean, NAP-level; if most siblings on the LCP also degrade, LCP-level; if most LCPs on the OLT degrade, OLT-level.
5. telemetry\_read: Rx and Tx per gateway on the hypothesised element over the last 2 hours, converted to dBm.
6. exposure\_calc: observed lines (orders or Handyman complaints), inferred lines (subscribers on the element with no contact), VIP/B2B count, MSF band total.
7. incident\_registry.create: write the object with state Proposed.
8. Rationale: one paragraph, in plain words, citing evidence IDs: order numbers, gateway MACs, SNOW number if any.

**Support score** (deterministic; shown as a support score, not a probability, until calibrated on adjudicated cases): weighted sum of ticket density relative to subscribers on the element, optical-drop share, reachability-loss share, sibling cleanliness, and alarm presence. Weights are a config file reviewed at Gate 0.

**Output object** (incident\_registry row): incident\_id, element\_type (NAP, LCP, OLT), element\_id, hypothesis\_rank (ranked candidates with score), observed\_lines, inferred\_lines, vip\_b2b\_count, severity (derived from lines affected using Globe's 5.x NAP-level versus 1 to 3 OLT-level framing), support\_score, evidence\_ids, rationale, state (Proposed, Confirmed, Rejected, Merged, Closed), proposed\_at, decided\_by, decided\_at.

**Human approver**: NOC / network-operations lead. Rejection captures a reason and becomes an evaluation case. Closure is proposed when optical returns to baseline and no new orders arrive for 30 minutes.

**What the demo shows**: a non-alarm NAP fault detected 48 minutes before the hourly manual cycle, the evidence trail, a merge of a duplicate cluster into an existing SNOW incident, and an honest abstain where the NAP is blank on the subscriber record (reported at cluster level, with the limitation stated).

**Evaluation at the pilot** (not in the demo): precision and recall against Globe-adjudicated historical incidents; detection lead time versus the ticket-only baseline.

## 5. Agent 2: Dispatch Reviewer (UC2)

The Reviewer looks at every dispatch-eligible work order before it rolls and recommends one of three outcomes with the evidence attached; the dispatcher decides, and the agent cannot cancel anything.

**Eligible population**: skillset = Repair, status in (Open, Unassigned, Pending) and appointmentdate within the next 24 hours. Orders already Cancelled, Completed or Ongoing are out of scope. INVALID, Cancelled and WithoutVisit are never counted as avoided rolls; Globe's draft answer on this is treated as a rule.

**The four questions, answered by tools, in order**

| Question | Tool | Evidence returned | Demo availability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is the line inside an open incident's blast radius? | incident\_registry\_read on dpid, lcpname, cabinetid | Incident ID, element, state, crew-assigned flag | Available |
| Is it inside planned works? | planned\_works\_check | Labelled UNAVAILABLE IN PILOT; the check runs and returns unknown, never clear | Unavailable, shown honestly |
| Is it inside a known power-outage footprint? | power\_footprint\_check | Same as planned works | Unavailable, shown honestly |
| Does the symptom signature point to a customer-side cause? | symptom\_signature\_check over Handyman (ModemStatus, LineStatus, PortStatus, optics Rx) and WMS (gateway Rx, reporting state) | CPE-only signature, equipment off, healthy optical with no neighbour impact | Available |

**Decision rules** (code, outside the agent)

- Suppress: line inside a Confirmed incident whose element is upstream of the line, and the incident has a crew assigned or an outage reference in SNOW. Evidence must cite the incident ID and at least one physical signal (optical drop or reachability loss) on the same element.
- Hold-and-recheck (N hours): line inside a Proposed incident, or the only evidence is a Handyman result older than 6 hours, or the gateway is not reporting but siblings are healthy. N defaults to 2.
- Dispatch: everything else, and always when the support score is below the Gate 0 confidence floor. The floor is a config value (0.80 in the demo). Below it, the suppress and hold branches are not reachable.
- Asymmetry: the cost model weights a wrong suppression five times a wrong dispatch, so the operating point sits where that asymmetry says it should. The demo shows the number.

**Output object** (dispatch\_recommendation row): workordernumber, outcome, reason\_class (shared upstream fault; planned work or power; customer, drop or CPE fault; duplicate dispatch; access refusal; insufficient evidence), evidence\_ids, support\_score, floor\_applied, estimated\_avoidable\_cost (unit cost is a placeholder until Finance supplies one), recommended\_at, dispatcher\_decision (accepted, overridden), override\_reason, outcome\_observed.

**Human approver**: the dispatcher, per territory. Every decision, including accepts, lands in adoption\_record.

**What the demo shows**: four suppressions on one new incident, one CPE-signature dispatch, one hold with a countdown, one override and its reason, and the unavailable checks drawn as unavailable. A second view shows what the same orders would look like under today's process (all dispatched within 24 hours).

**Evaluation at the pilot**: suppression precision on adjudicated cases, with the safety-critical class (suppressed or held, but field repair was needed) tracked on its own.

## 6. Agent 3: Queue Prioritizer (UC3)

The Prioritizer keeps the open-work queue ranked by a scoring function the operations lead can read, re-ranks on events, and explains any position on demand. No language model ranks anything.

**Queue members**: Confirmed and Proposed incidents from incident\_registry, plus dispatch-eligible orders not inside any incident.

**Score** (arithmetic, weights in config, set with Globe at Gate 0; demo defaults in brackets)

```latex
S = w_t \cdot \min\left(\frac{t_{elapsed}}{6h}, 1.5\right) + w_l \cdot \log_{10}(1 + lines) + w_s \cdot sev + w_v \cdot vip + w_c \cdot (1 - crew)
```

- t\_elapsed from the earliest createdate in the cluster (the clock definition is itself a Gate 0 item). \[w\_t = 0.40\]
- lines = observed plus inferred lines on the element. \[w\_l = 0.25\]
- sev = 1.0 for OLT-level, 0.6 for LCP, 0.3 for NAP, 0.1 for single line. \[w\_s = 0.20\]
- vip = share of VIP/B2B lines; only used if Globe approves a commercial rule, otherwise w\_v = 0. The demo shows the toggle off, and shows what changes if it is on. \[w\_v = 0 by default\]
- crew = 1 if a crew is assigned (static team field), else 0. Live crew availability is unavailable in the pilot and is shown as such. \[w\_c = 0.15\]

**Behaviour**: re-rank on incident create, confirm, close; on crew assignment change; every 15 minutes regardless. Flag an exception when a lower-severity item will breach its 6-hour window before a higher-severity item does.

**Output object** (queue\_rank row): item\_id, item\_type, rank, score, each input value, breach\_eta, exception\_flag, exception\_text, ranked\_at.

**Conversational answers** (the role-scoped surface): why is X above Y; what should my team take next; what breaches in the next two hours. Each answer lists the inputs and cites the queue\_rank row.

**Human approver**: the operations lead owns the policy; weights change only through change control. In the demo the weights are editable in a sandbox panel so the audience can see the queue react, with a note that production weights are locked.

**What the demo shows**: a new NAP incident overtaking an older OLT incident that already has a crew, the breach-time exception, and the explanation on demand.

**Evaluation at the pilot**: rank-order agreement with Globe-adjudicated cases of what should have run first; window-breach anticipation lead time.

## 7. Agent 4: Customer Notification (UC5)

The Notification agent turns a Confirmed incident into a draft, policy-checked customer notification package routed to Globe's communications approver; in the demo nothing is sent, and the single governed write (NG1 handoff) is shown disabled.

**Trigger**: incident state becomes Confirmed, or an existing Confirmed incident's restoration target changes, or 2 hours pass since the last update on an open incident.

**Behaviour**

1. subscriber\_resolve: all subscribers on the element from the postpaid and prepaid bases joined on nap, plus WMS subscriber keys; split observed (called or ran a Handyman check) from inferred (silent). The inferred set is the value of the use case.
2. Segment: VIP/B2B versus mass (synthetic flag in the demo; Globe's flag is unconfirmed).
3. restoration\_target\_read: Globe's published target for the incident if outage management has one; otherwise the message carries acknowledgement and a next-update time. The platform never computes its own ETA.
4. contact\_policy\_check per recipient: quiet hours (22:00 to 07:00 local), frequency cap (one message per incident per 2 hours), cross-incident dedupe (one message per customer per experience), consent and opt-out (UNAVAILABLE IN PILOT, so the package is draft-only by rule).
5. notification\_package\_build: template-based text in English (non-English is out of scope), recipient count by segment, timing, evidence IDs; identifiers stay pseudonymised in the package. Re-identification would happen only inside Globe at the point of contact.
6. Route to the communications approver; on approval, the demo displays the NG1 handoff call it would make, with the send button disabled and the reason stated.
7. reply\_capture\_read: shown as a placeholder panel for m360 delivery and reply signal, labelled as pending the m360/ISDP decision.

**Output object** (notification\_package row): package\_id, incident\_id, recipients\_observed, recipients\_inferred, recipients\_vip, text, restoration\_target or next\_update\_at, policy\_results per check, state (Draft, Approved, Withheld, Sent), approver, decided\_at.

**Human approver**: Globe communications. No approval, no send; there is no bypass path.

**What the demo shows**: 14 recipients on the confirmed NAP incident, 10 of them silent; the policy panel with consent marked unavailable; the approver view; the disabled handoff. Then a second incident where quiet hours hold the package until 07:00.

**Evaluation at the pilot**: update reliability, reach into the silent set, reply-measured informedness, and zero contact-policy violations.

**Evidence trail convention**: every card carries an evidence drawer. **Conversational panel**: available on every screen for the role's scope. It answers only through registered tools over the curated layer; the demo includes three canned questions and one free question from the audience.

**Design**: DevX Doctrine styling (one accent, hairline rules, Inter Tight), dark mode off, 1440 px desktop layout, no mobile.

**Guardrails as code, outside the agents**

- Suppression floor: below 0.80 support score, the suppress and hold outcomes are unreachable branches.
- Grounding contract: every consequential output must reference resolvable evidence IDs and satisfy its typed schema, or it abstains. The UI cannot render an ungrounded answer.
- Untrusted text: Handyman free text, fixdescription, finddescription, inc\_short\_description and customer text are data, never instructions. Prompt-injection, tool-confusion and cross-role isolation tests are part of the demo's regression suite, and one injection attempt is shown live (a fixdescription containing an instruction, ignored).
- PII: no names or phone numbers exist in the demo data. Account and service IDs are hashed. Re-identification is a Globe-side step and is drawn as such.
- Missing-data honesty: a stalled feed raises an alert on the feed-health strip and blocks detection on that source; it is never read as no incidents. The demo stalls the Handyman feed for ten minutes to show it.
- Effective-dated topology: the demo includes one NAP re-parented mid-day; historical attribution stays with the old parent.
- Contact policy enforced by tool, not by agent judgement.

**Human-in-the-loop matrix**

| Output | Approver | On rejection or override |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incident isolation | NOC / network-operations lead | Reason captured; evaluation case created; incident re-investigated |
| Dispatch suppression or hold | Dispatcher | Override reason and outcome captured in adoption\_record |
| Queue ranking policy and weights | Operations lead | Weights change only through change control; sandbox is non-persistent |
| Customer notification package | Communications team | Package revised or withheld; nothing is sent unapproved; no send path in the demo |

**Full trace**: every agent run logs inputs, tool calls with parameters, outputs, approver, decision and outcome to an append-only log; the demo shows the trace for one incident end to end.

## 11. Architecture for the demo build, and the path to the pilot

The demo is the pilot's three-plane design on a laptop-sized footprint: the same tool registry, the same typed outputs, the same approver matrix, with DuckDB standing in for BigQuery and a hosted single-page app standing in for the Gemini Enterprise surface.

&#91;embedded content: demo architecture: three planes, four agents, one surface\]

Data flows upward only through tools; decisions flow back down into the adoption record, which the surface reads. Nothing on the agent plane can reach a source table directly.

**Demo stack**

| Layer | Demo build | Pilot (weeks 5 to 12) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storage | DuckDB over the generated CSV and parquet; same table names as the curated layer | BigQuery in Globe's tenant, authorized views, GQI convergence |
| Detectors and tools | Python services, one module per registered tool, typed with pydantic; deterministic | Same code, deployed on Cloud Run inside the tenant |
| Agents | Google ADK agents on Gemini, tool-calling only, no free-form data access; A2A between agents | Same, registered in Gemini Enterprise with role scoping |
| Surface | React single-page app, hosted; DevX Doctrine styling | Gemini Enterprise surface plus the same screens |
| Trace | Append-only JSON log per run | Cloud Audit Logs to a retention-locked bucket; BigQuery analytical sink |
| Replay clock | The synthetic day plays at 10x with pause and scrub, so a 20-minute demo covers the morning | Live clock |

**What carries over unchanged at Gate 2**: the schemas, the tool registry, the output objects, the guardrail code, the evaluation harness shape. What is replaced: the data (synthetic to curated Globe data), the storage engine, the clock, and the surface host. That is the point of building the demo on Globe's own column names.

## 12. Non-goals, open questions, acceptance criteria

**Non-goals for the demo**

- No live connection to FSM, ServiceNow, Handyman, WMS or any NMS; no API integration of any kind.
- No Globe figures: no baseline, no savings claim, no improvement percentage. Every number on screen comes from the seeded day.
- No trained predictive model; detection and scoring are deterministic rules plus an LLM orchestration and explanation layer, as the SOW commits.
- No send path, no NG1 call, no m360 integration; UC5 ends at an approved draft.
- No mobile layout, no non-English text, no workforce scheduling or crew routing.
- No raw-alarm correlation; the alarm sample is corroboration only.

**Acceptance criteria** (the demo is ready when all are true)

1. The six scenarios in section 10 play end to end on the replay clock without manual intervention, twice in a row, from the seeded generator.
2. Every card on every screen opens an evidence drawer that lists real row identifiers from the synthetic tables, and no rendered answer lacks one.
3. With the support score forced below 0.80, no suppress or hold outcome appears anywhere in the dispatch review; this is verified by a test, not by inspection.
4. The feed-stall scenario raises an alert within one replay interval and clears when the feed resumes; no incident is created from the stalled source during the stall.
5. The prompt-injection string in the seeded fixdescription produces no change in any recommendation and is visible in the trace as ignored data.
6. The UC5 package shows consent as unavailable and the send control as disabled with the reason; no code path calls ng1\_handoff.
7. Every screen loads in under two seconds on the demo laptop; the free question from the audience answers in under 15 seconds through registered tools.
8. Globe's column names in the schemas match the handoff dictionaries exactly; a diff against the dictionary files is part of the build.
9. A Globe reviewer (control tower lead) has walked the dry run and agreed the morning reads as their operation.
